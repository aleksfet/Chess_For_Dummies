Learn the Game

Here are the basics you need to take your first steps in chess.

What Is Chess and How Does It Work?

Chess is a two-player strategy game played on a 64-square board. White moves first, and each side starts with 16 pieces that each move in their own way. The main goal is to checkmate the opponent's king, meaning the king is under attack and has no safe escape. Games can also end by resignation, timeout, stalemate, or draw.

What makes chess fun is the strategy behind every move. Players create plans, attack and defend, set traps, win material, promote pawns, and use tactics like forks, pins, and checkmates to outthink their opponent. You can play chess on a physical board with friends or in tournaments, and you can also play online through websites and apps.

To understand how chess became one of the world's most famous strategy board games — and to get more familiar with common chess terms — view its history and key terms below.

Chess History & Terms

Chess has ancient roots, growing out of early strategy games played many centuries ago. Over time, the rules changed and the modern version of chess we know today gradually took shape.

As it spread from region to region, chess became a global competitive game, with champions, clubs, and worldwide tournaments. Today it is enjoyed in every way imaginable — casually with friends, online through apps and websites, in local clubs, and in major international tournaments.

Key Terms

Checkmate
The king is under attack and has no legal way to escape. This ends the game and wins it.
Stalemate
A player has no legal moves but their king is not in check. The game ends in a draw.
Blunder
A bad move that loses material or the game. Everyone makes them — spotting yours helps you improve.
Fork
One piece attacks two or more enemy pieces at the same time, so your opponent can't save them all.
Pin
A piece is stuck in place because moving it would expose a more valuable piece behind it.
Promotion
When a pawn reaches the far side of the board, it turns into a stronger piece — usually a queen.
Elo
A number that estimates a player's strength. It goes up when you win and down when you lose.

Openings, Endgames, and Improvement

Chess is not just about memorizing moves. It is a deep strategy game built on understanding patterns, plans, and positions. Players improve by learning openings, studying endgames, solving tactics, playing practice games, reviewing mistakes, and building better study habits over time.

Openings Shape the Game

Openings are the first moves of a chess game. They help you control the center, develop your pieces, and keep your king safe, while choosing the kind of position you want to play. As a beginner, learn the simple opening principles first — there's no need to memorize long lines yet.

Endgames Teach You How to Finish

The endgame is the phase when only a few pieces remain. Studying king activity, pawn endings, rook endings, promotion, and basic checkmates teaches you how to turn a small edge into a win and finish winning positions cleanly.

Tactics and Study Habits

Tactics and puzzles build pattern recognition, training your eye to spot ideas like forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks, and checkmates. Good study habits make this stick: solve puzzles consistently, read chess books, watch instructional videos, and learn from stronger players.

Practice Games, Online Elo, and Review

Playing online games on Chess.com or Lichess helps you gain real experience, and your online Elo rating gives a rough way to track your progress. Reviewing and analyzing your own games helps you understand your mistakes and improve. Online chess offers different formats and is fun, user-friendly, and easy to practice with.

OTB Chess and Ratings

Understanding real-world chess and how skill is measured.

What is OTB Chess?

OTB means "over the board" — playing chess in person with a physical board, instead of online. It's the classic way to compete.

USCF and Tournaments

The US Chess Federation runs official tournaments in America. Joining lets you earn an official rating and play rated games.

Elo Ratings

The Elo system gives every player a number that reflects their strength. Win against stronger players and your rating climbs.

FIDE and International Chess

FIDE is the world chess organization. It awards titles like Grandmaster and oversees international competition worldwide.

Chess Legends

Some of the greatest and most influential players in history.

MC

Magnus Carlsen

World Champion & All-Time Great

Widely considered the strongest player ever, Magnus reached the world's highest rating. He is famous for grinding out wins from quiet positions.

GK

Garry Kasparov

Dominant World Champion

Kasparov ruled the chess world for nearly two decades. His aggressive style and famous match against the computer Deep Blue made him a legend.

BF

Bobby Fischer

American Genius

Fischer became a global icon by winning the 1972 World Championship. His brilliance helped spark huge interest in chess around the world.

JP

Judit Polgar

Greatest Female Player Ever

Judit Polgar shattered records and beat many top male players in her career. She proved chess greatness has nothing to do with gender.

HN

Hikaru Nakamura

Grandmaster & Streamer

A top grandmaster and speed-chess wizard, Hikaru introduced millions of new fans to the game through his popular streams and videos.
